Lasting Power of Attorney
A simulation · Singapore
Five phases · Fifty years

One hundred people

An LPA is signed while you still have capacity — that is the whole point of it. Watch a hundred lives run forward fifty years, and see how much gentler it is for the families of the people who took an hour to sign one.

One hundred people, all aged forty, all well. Every phase moves them ten years on. Some will die. Some will lose the capacity to make their own decisions, through dementia, stroke or injury. Nobody knows in advance which square is theirs — which is exactly why the document exists.

The one thing you can change here is how many signed an LPA at the start, before anything happened. It changes nothing about who falls ill and nothing about who dies. It changes only what the people who love them have to do next: act on the authority they were given, or apply to the Family Justice Courts for it.

What the two routes cost
Signed in advance

S$0 + a witness fee

Since 1 April 2026 the registration fee for LPA Form 1 is permanently waived for Singapore citizens. You still pay a certificate issuer to witness and certify it — among the most-visited accredited practitioners that has typically been around S$50 or less. One appointment, three to six weeks at the OPG, no annual fee, and the donee is someone you chose yourself.

Applied for afterwards

S$5,000–S$15,000

With no LPA, the family applies to the Family Justice Courts for a deputyship order under the Mental Capacity Act. Indicative legal costs run from S$5,000 to S$15,000, six to twelve months is common before anyone can act, and the deputy reports to the Public Guardian every year after that.
